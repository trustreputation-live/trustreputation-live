What Are Trustpilot Invitation Services?

Trustpilot invitation services are the mechanisms that allow businesses to systematically ask their customers to leave a review after a purchase or service experience. Rather than hoping reviews appear organically, these tools automate — or at least streamline — the process of reaching customers at the right moment.

Trustpilot permits all businesses to invite customers, free of charge, provided the invitations go out in a fair and neutral way. The platform's rules are clear: you must invite all customers, not just the ones you think will say something nice. Cherry-picking is against the guidelines and can get your account flagged.

💡 Key Principle

Invitation services are about making it easy for your real customers to share their genuine experience. Volume comes from consistency — not from shortcuts. The businesses with the strongest Trustpilot profiles simply make review requests a standard part of their post-purchase process.

The Four Core Trustpilot Invitation Methods

Trustpilot offers a range of invitation options — from fully automated to entirely manual. Here's a clear breakdown of each.

⚙️

Automatic Feedback Service (AFS) Most Popular

You BCC a unique Trustpilot email address into your transactional emails. Trustpilot reads the customer data and automatically queues a review invitation a few days after purchase. No developer required.

🔌

Platform Integrations Easy Setup

Plug-and-play connectors for Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento, BigCommerce, Salesforce, and more. Invitations fire automatically based on order events — ideal for e-commerce businesses.

🧑‍💻

API Invitations Advanced

A direct server-to-server connection that gives developers full control over when, how, and to whom invitations are sent. Supports both service and product review flows simultaneously.

📋

Manual Invitations Free Tier

Upload a CSV of customer data or copy-paste contacts directly into Trustpilot. Reviews collected this way are labelled "Invited" rather than "Verified" — but it's a solid starting point.

Setting Up Automatic Feedback Service (AFS) Step by Step

AFS is the most widely used invitation method for a reason: it takes about ten minutes to configure, it's included in Trustpilot's free plan, and once it's running, it works in the background without any manual effort. Here's how to get it live.

Log into Trustpilot Business and navigate to invitations

Go to Get Reviews → Invitation Methods → Trustpilot Automatic Invitations (AFS). You'll find your unique BCC email address here — something like 52bxxxx93e@invite.trustpilot.com. Copy it carefully.

Add the BCC address to your transactional emails

In your email platform, add this address as a BCC on your order confirmation or shipping notification emails. Trustpilot recommends triggering from the shipping or delivery email rather than the order confirmation — customers who haven't received their order yet can't leave a meaningful review.

Add the structured data snippet (optional but recommended)

If you want the invitation to include the customer's name, order reference, or specific products, add Trustpilot's JSON structured data snippet to your email template. AFS can pull basic data from the subject line and "To" field without it, but the snippet improves personalisation.

Set your invitation delay

Trustpilot defaults to sending the review request 7 days after receiving your BCC. For most service businesses, this is fine. For physical product deliveries, consider extending to 10–14 days to ensure the parcel has arrived before the customer is asked to review.

Enable reminder emails

Inside Trustpilot Business, switch on reminder emails. These are a single, friendly follow-up to customers who received the first invitation but didn't respond. On average, reminders generate 35% additional reviews at no extra effort.

Verify using the invitation history log

Check your invitation history in Trustpilot Business a few days after setup. You should start seeing invitations queued and sent. If nothing appears, your email client may be stripping the BCC field — check your email system's settings or consult your ESP's documentation.

Using Platform Integrations for E-Commerce

If your business runs on Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento, or another major e-commerce platform, you may not need to touch the AFS BCC approach at all. Trustpilot has built native integrations for these platforms that handle the entire invitation flow automatically.

These integrations connect your order management system directly to Trustpilot. When an order is marked as delivered or completed, the integration triggers a review invitation — no BCC required, no manual steps. For businesses processing large volumes of orders, this is almost always the cleanest setup.

Find these under Get Reviews → App Directory in your Trustpilot Business account. Most integrations require a paid Trustpilot subscription and webhook access, so check your plan before starting.

Collecting Reviews Across the Entire Customer Journey

Most businesses treat review invitations as a single post-purchase event. The smarter approach is to think across the full customer journey and identify the right moments for different types of feedback.

AFS supports multiple trigger points. You can configure separate invitation flows for:

✓

Post-purchase — service review immediately after the transaction is confirmed

✓

Post-delivery — product review once shipping is confirmed as delivered

✓

Post-support — service review after a customer care ticket is resolved

✓

Service completion — for trades, consultancies, or subscription renewals

Each of these touchpoints gives customers the chance to leave feedback that is genuinely relevant to that part of their experience. This also gives your profile a more rounded, authentic look — a mix of review types at different stages, rather than a wall of near-identical post-checkout comments.

Invitation Templates, Branding, and Reminder Timing

The email your customer receives matters. A badly timed, generic invitation gets ignored. A well-crafted, on-brand message from a business they recognise gets clicked.

Trustpilot's built-in invitation templates are available in 16 languages and can be customised with your logo, brand colours, and tone of voice. If you use a paid plan, you can also create custom templates from scratch and A/B test them against each other.

What makes a good review invitation?

✓

Use the customer's first name — personalisation increases open and click rates meaningfully

✓

Reference the specific order or service — it anchors the request in a real experience

✓

Keep the copy neutral — never ask for a "positive" or "great" review; ask for an honest one

✓

Send from a recognisable sender name — your brand name, not "noreply@" addresses that feel like spam

✓

One clear call-to-action — a single "Leave a review" button, nothing competing with it

Best Practices for Staying Compliant With Trustpilot's Guidelines

Trustpilot has significantly tightened its platform rules over the past few years. Businesses that bend — or break — the invitation rules face profile penalties, review removal, or outright suspension. None of those outcomes are worth the short-term gain.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

Don't send invitations only to customers you expect will leave positive feedback. Don't offer incentives (discounts, gifts, loyalty points) in exchange for reviews. Don't use third-party services that generate fake or fabricated reviews. Trustpilot's fraud detection systems are sophisticated and improving constantly — and the consequences of being caught are severe.

The most sustainable strategy is also the simplest: invite every customer, at the right time, with a neutral message. Let the volume of genuine feedback do the work. Businesses with 200 honest reviews — some three-star, some five-star — appear far more credible to consumers than profiles with 50 uniform five-star comments.

Getting Your First Reviews: The Bulk Invitation Strategy

If you're just setting up your Trustpilot profile, you may feel like you're starting from nothing. Trustpilot has a solution for this: bulk historical invitations allow you to contact customers from the past 90 days and ask them to review experiences they've already had.

This is a one-time mechanism — you can't repeatedly re-import the same customers — but it's a legitimate and effective way to seed your profile before your AFS automation kicks in. Upload a CSV of recent customers, send the invitations, and let the responses come in over the following days.

💡 Pro Tip

Run your bulk historical invite the same week you complete your AFS setup. That way, you get an initial volume boost from past customers while your automated system begins collecting reviews from new ones simultaneously.

Why Trustpilot Reviews Now Matter for AI Search Too

The conversation around Trustpilot reviews used to be almost entirely about Google Search visibility and consumer trust on the platform itself. That's still true — but there's a newer reason to prioritise your review volume: AI-powered search.

Search engines powered by AI, including Google's AI Overviews and other generative search tools, are increasingly pulling third-party review data into their responses when users ask questions like "is [company] reliable?" or "best [service] in the UK." A well-populated Trustpilot profile — with recent, varied, verified reviews — signals credibility to these systems in a way that self-published website content simply cannot.

Fresh reviews matter here more than old ones. A profile with 40 reviews from 2022 and nothing since reads as dormant. Automated invitation services keep your profile active and current, which is exactly what both human readers and AI engines are looking for.
