Understanding Trustpilot's Review Invitation Subscription

Published August 21, 2026 · 6 min read

For any business that sells online, customer reviews have become one of the strongest signals of trust a shopper looks for before making a purchase. Trustpilot, one of the most widely used review platforms, has built an entire feature set around helping businesses collect those reviews systematically rather than hoping customers leave feedback on their own. That feature is called review invitations, and it is delivered through Trustpilot's paid business subscription plans. This article explains what the invitation system is, how it works, and what businesses get at each subscription tier.

What is a Trustpilot review invitation?

A review invitation is an automated request, usually sent by email, asking a customer to rate a recent purchase or experience. Instead of relying on customers to visit a company's Trustpilot profile unprompted, the business triggers an invitation shortly after an order or interaction is completed. This turns review collection into a predictable, ongoing process rather than a one-off effort.

How the invitation system works

Trustpilot offers a few different ways to send these invitations:

  • Automatic invitations — connected through an e-commerce or CRM integration (such as Shopify, WooCommerce, HubSpot, or Salesforce) so that an invitation goes out automatically after a purchase or ticket is closed.
  • Invitation links or API-based invitations — a generated link a business can insert into its own transactional emails, useful for companies with a custom checkout or email system.
  • Customizable templates — invitation emails can be translated and styled to match the company's branding, available in multiple languages.
  • Reminder emails — a follow-up sent to customers who haven't responded to the first invitation, which Trustpilot reports can meaningfully increase response rates.
The number of invitations a business can send each month, and whether reminder emails and an in-app review collector are included, depends on which subscription plan the business is on.

Why businesses subscribe to it

A steady stream of recent, genuine reviews does two things for a business: it builds trust with prospective customers browsing the company's profile, and it gives the company structured feedback it can act on. Because review volume tends to correlate with buyer confidence, many businesses treat the invitation subscription as a growth and reputation-management tool rather than just a customer-service add-on.

Things to keep in mind

Review platforms generally require that invitations only be sent to real customers who actually completed a transaction, and that businesses not selectively invite only satisfied customers while excluding dissatisfied ones. Following these guidelines keeps the review profile authentic and compliant with the platform's terms of service.
